# Internet sources policy

Chara may gather one small source-based idea per day, but only if it improves the email. No fear-based news feed.

## Preferred source types

Use calm, practical, verifiable sources:

- official medical organizations;
- university or hospital explainers;
- PubMed abstracts or review articles;
- sleep medicine organizations;
- behavioral science sources with practical relevance;
- reputable clinical or public health sources.

## Allowed topics

- sleep hygiene and circadian rhythm;
- caffeine timing;
- daylight and morning routine;
- work shutdown rituals;
- stress externalization and task capture;
- movement breaks for sedentary work;
- recovery after poor sleep;
- vocal practice, breathing, and creative recovery, with cautious wording.

## Avoid

- alarming health headlines;
- mortality statistics as motivation;
- influencer advice;
- body transformation content;
- comparison stories;
- restrictive diet trends;
- supplement claims;
- medical treatment suggestions.

## Required framing

Source-based item must be converted into Yuriy's language.

Bad:

"A study says poor sleep increases disease risk. Fix your sleep."

Good:

"Today's useful idea: morning light helps anchor the body clock. This will not solve sleep breathing, but it can make the rhythm variable cleaner. Small action: five minutes of outdoor light or a bright window in the first half hour after waking."

## Citation rule

If an email uses an internet source, include source name and link. Do not quote long passages.
