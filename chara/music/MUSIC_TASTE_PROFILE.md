# Yuriy — music taste profile (working hypothesis)

Status: provisional. This is an interpretation layer derived from a small seed playlist, not a fixed identity statement.

## What the current seed strongly suggests

The current tracks lean toward emotionally charged, atmospheric songwriting rather than bright, lightweight pop.

Repeated qualities:

- dark or nocturnal mood;
- restrained verses that can open into larger emotional peaks;
- strong, characterful lead vocals;
- minor-key / melancholic harmonic color;
- cinematic or spacious production;
- songs with tension, longing, sensuality, gravity, or dramatic release;
- alternative / indie adjacency rather than purely chart-pop aesthetics;
- memorable melodic songs that still leave room for texture and atmosphere.

Representative seed examples:

- David Usher — `Black Black Heart`
- Chris Isaak — `Wicked Game`
- Blue Foundation — `Eyes on Fire`
- Sting — `Shape Of My Heart`
- Hozier — `Take Me To Church`

## Likely useful recommendation directions

Prioritize recommendations with one or more of these properties:

- expressive male or female vocals with a distinctive timbre;
- dramatic but not bombastic arrangements;
- live performances where interpretation changes the song;
- songs with a clear emotional arc;
- guitar- or piano-led material that can be enjoyed without technical virtuosity;
- familiar-song energy: something Yuriy could plausibly want to sing or play, not an exercise disguised as inspiration;
- interesting covers that reveal a song from a darker, more intimate, or more cinematic angle.

## Avoid as default morning prompts

Do not turn recommendations into tests of musicianship.

Avoid prompts such as:

- improvise over this;
- pick this out by ear;
- transcribe the chords or melody;
- identify the harmony;
- learn a technically difficult passage;
- prove or measure a skill.

These can convert curiosity into a competence check and reduce motivation.

Better pattern:

1. Surface one concrete musical object: song, live take, cover, vocal moment, arrangement choice, old familiar track.
2. Say briefly what is interesting about it.
3. Invite listening first.
4. If appropriate, offer only a low-friction continuation using already familiar material: sing along, play a known song once, revisit an old recording.
5. An action is optional. The primary goal is to make music mentally present again.
