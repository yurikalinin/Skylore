# Yuriy — music taste profile (working hypothesis)

Status: provisional. This is an interpretation layer for music discovery and morning recommendations, not a fixed identity statement.

## Important interpretation rule

A playlist is **not automatically a list of favorite artists or a pure taste sample**.

Yuriy often builds playlists for a purpose: a game/session atmosphere, exercise, writing, nostalgia, singing, or another activity. Context can explain a large cluster of tracks better than genre preference.

When inferring taste, distinguish:

- music chosen because Yuriy actively likes the song/artist;
- music chosen because it fits an activity or fictional world;
- music kept because of autobiographical memory;
- music saved as possible singing/guitar material;
- novelty/covers/remixes saved because the transformation itself is fun.

Do not flatten these categories into one genre profile.

## Current high-confidence tendencies

### 1. Direct emotional access matters more than musical prestige

Yuriy responds readily to tenderness, nostalgia, energy, humor, melody, narrative and recognizable emotional gestures.

He does not need music to be harmonically or structurally complex to value it. Do not frame recommendations around sophistication, connoisseurship, difficulty, or "important music".

### 2. Heavy music is a real preference, especially as energy music

Metal / hard rock / industrial-adjacent material is a substantial part of the library and is especially useful for physical-energy contexts.

This does **not** imply a general preference for dark, depressive or oppressive music.

### 3. Very dark music can be aversive rather than attractive

Do not assume that melancholy = desirable. Some dark vocal/atmospheric music can feel emotionally overwhelming and should not be the default discovery direction.

Likewise, do not default to art rock, Radiohead-like introspection, jazz, or difficult/opaque music simply because it is critically respected.

### 4. Covers, transformations and musical jokes are unusually productive discovery territory

The library contains many metal covers, genre swaps, remixes, choir versions, folk versions and deliberately absurd reinterpretations.

A familiar song transformed into a surprising new style is often a better recommendation hook than "an acclaimed artist you should know".

### 5. Music can be world-building

Several playlists are built to make games or imagined settings more vivid: folk/medieval material, retro dance/pop, soundtracks, cyber/retro aesthetics and other contextual selections.

For discovery, it can be useful to ask: "What world does this song open?" rather than only "What genre is it?"

### 6. Nostalgia is associative and powerful

Some tracks function as autobiographical triggers. Familiar recordings from childhood or old media can carry disproportionate emotional value regardless of current genre preferences.

Treat nostalgia as its own recommendation category, not evidence that Yuriy generally prefers one decade or style.

### 7. Listening sometimes contains a performer impulse

Yuriy often catches himself wanting to be able to **sing** something he hears.

This is a useful morning-music hook: surface songs or performances that might create "I want to sing that" desire.

Do not turn that desire into a musicianship test.

Avoid prompts such as:

- improvise over this;
- pick this out by ear;
- transcribe the chords or melody;
- identify the harmony;
- prove vocal range or technique;
- learn a technically difficult passage before enjoyment is allowed.

Better sequence:

1. Surface one concrete musical object: song, live take, cover, vocal moment, arrangement, or familiar track.
2. Say briefly what is interesting or emotionally distinctive about it.
3. Invite listening first.
4. If it sparks performer desire, optionally make continuation easy: familiar chords, karaoke/minus, known song, or simply sing along.
5. No action is required. The first goal is to make music mentally present again.

## Poetry / performance crossover

Modern Russian poetry is a promising adjacent discovery area.

Yuriy has used poetry inside karaoke performances, reading a fitting poem during instrumental sections. Treat this not as a "cheat" to correct, but as a legitimate performance device: music can provide an emotional frame for spoken text.

Future recommendations may occasionally pair:

- a poem with a song or instrumental mood;
- a poet with a performance idea;
- a short text that could live inside an instrumental break.

Do not force this into every music recommendation.

## Morning recommendation modes

Useful modes include:

- **Want to sing this?** — a song with immediate performer appeal;
- **Transformation** — a familiar piece in an unexpected form;
- **World opening** — music that instantly creates a setting or era;
- **Memory key** — an older familiar track or nearby discovery with strong nostalgia potential;
- **Energy** — direct, physical, high-impact music;
- **Tenderness** — emotionally warm material without oppressive darkness;
- **Poetry bridge** — an occasional poem/song pairing for spoken performance.

## What remains uncertain

Do not infer without new explicit evidence:

- fixed favorite artists;
- a single dominant genre;
- preferred decade;
- sophisticated-vs-simple identity labels;
- whether a playlist track was chosen for personal taste or for context;
- which songs are realistic current vocal repertoire;
- what Yuriy most likes playing on guitar.

Update this profile when new evidence is explicit. Do not force new music to fit the existing theory.
